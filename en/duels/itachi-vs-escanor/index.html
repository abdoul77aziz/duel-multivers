Canon boundaries

Use condition
The Totsuka still has to connect

The Sword of Totsuka seals for eternity whatever it pierces — in theory, even a divinity. But in practice it is a blade like any other: limited reach, a trajectory that can be dodged. Facing an Escanor capable of subduing an opponent of Meliodas's caliber with a single gesture, the obstacle isn't the blade's nature — it's landing it before the exchange turns to the Lion's advantage.

Major gray area
Amaterasu against a body that is itself a sun

The black flames burn everything they touch for seven days, with no heat source known in Naruto able to extinguish them. But Escanor himself generates enough heat to melt blades at a distance: nothing proves a body of solar nature would resist flames of another category, nothing proves the opposite either. What remains is a scenario the gray zone opens rather than closes: a struck Escanor would probably keep fighting out of pure pride, refusing to admit defeat as long as The One hasn't worn off.

Canon precision
Amaterasu doesn't need what Tsukuyomi needs

This fiche's central tension is built around Tsukuyomi's demanding condition: Escanor has to look directly into Itachi's eyes for it to trigger. Amaterasu carries no such requirement. Canon is specific about the difference: Tsukuyomi needs direct eye contact with the target, while Amaterasu only needs Itachi to have the target within his own line of sight — no reciprocal gaze, nothing required from Escanor's side at all. That doesn't resolve the heat-resistance gray area above, but it means the black flames are a far easier card to attempt than the headline technique, available from the first exchange rather than gated behind a fragile eye-lock.

Itachi's card — and its limit
Clones are a diversion, not a solution to the gaze

Itachi is a seasoned user of Kage Bunshin: saturating space, feinting a charge, forcing Escanor to split his attention are all perfectly canonical uses. What nothing confirms, however, is that a clone can trigger a Tsukuyomi in the original's place — the technique passes through Itachi's own eyes, and a clone dissolves at the first hit it takes. Kage Bunshin buy time and confusion; they don't buy the gaze.

The verdict

The table gives Escanor the edge without ever crushing Itachi (46 against 49 out of 70) — and that is exactly the spirit of this duel. Itachi does not lose for lack of tools: he loses because his true win condition, Tsukuyomi, rests on a wager that Escanor's canon has already partly denied, and that Itachi's own canon never presented as absolute.

A lion who has already withstood the most dangerous mental intrusion in his own universe is no longer the ideal genjutsu target, even an exceptional one — but the margin stays narrow, not crushing.

Nearly half the scenarios still belong to Itachi: the ones where the gaze lands before the charge, where the Totsuka connects in that fraction-of-a-second window, or where Amaterasu proves more devastating than a solar power can withstand. It is not an impassable wall for Konoha — it is a narrow door, and it has to open on the first try, because Escanor won't offer a second one.
