The book

Twenty impossible fights

What if the biggest characters in anime and manga fought outside their own universes?

From Naruto to Luffy, from Gojo to Whitebeard, Duels in the Multiverse of Madness stages twenty impossible fights by weighing each combatant's abilities, techniques and weaknesses against what their own story has actually shown.

This isn't a raw power contest: every duel turns on a rule, an incompatibility, or one detail that flips the outcome. Twenty fights, nineteen verdicts… and one final question left for the reader.

The question we get asked first

Why pay when scans are free?

It's a fair question, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a sales pitch.

This book doesn't replace the source material. It's for winning the argument that comes after.

Scans give you the story. They don't give you the arguments. When the conversation turns to "Luffy Gear 5 beats Baryon Mode Naruto," nobody opens a scan — people look for evidence, specific scenes, a chain of logic that survives three minutes of pushback.

This book's real competition was never the manga. It's the twenty-minute "who wins" videos that contradict each other, invent feats nobody has ever seen, and can't be cited or checked.

A written method, not an opinion

Locked snapshots, demonstrated feats only, no chained scaling. The rules are stated up front and held for all twenty duels. When canon itself doesn't settle something, a box says so — instead of making it up.

A document, not a video

159 pages you can cite, reread, and send mid-argument. An argument buried in a twenty-minute video isn't an argument — it's a hazy memory.

Checked against the source, not against fandom memory

Every canon claim here was checked against the original work itself, not against the vague "I'm pretty sure that happened" the internet usually runs on. Where the source doesn't settle something, the book says so instead of guessing.

What €3.99 actually buys

Less than a single coffee, once — against the mobile data you'd burn watching three videos that give you three different answers, and no source between them. (2,000 FCFA via Mobile Money for readers in Africa.)

The method

Four rules, held twenty times

The feat

An accomplishment shown on screen or on the page. If a character says someone else could destroy a continent, that's not a feat — that's dialogue.

The snapshot

Each sheet locks one precise moment. Luffy appears twice in this book — before the timeskip and in Gear 5 — and they're two different fighters.

No chained scaling

"A beat B, and B once hurt C, so A beats C" is banned here. A fight is won on conditions, not on a leaderboard.

The victory clause

Incapacitation? Definitive death? A seal? It's stated before every duel — and some of them hinge entirely on that one line.
